# Unit 1: 3D Representations

## Overview
This unit explores the various ways to represent 3D objects and scenes in computer graphics and machine learning, focusing on both traditional and AI-friendly formats.

## What You'll Learn
- Different methods for representing 3D data
- The strengths and limitations of each representation
- How these representations fit into modern 3D AI pipelines
- The relationship between research-friendly and application-ready formats

## Contents
- `introduction.md` - Introduction to 3D representations and their importance
- `meshes.md` - Traditional mesh representation and its challenges for ML
- `non-meshes.md` - Alternative 3D representations used in AI research
- `pipelines.md` - How different representations work together in 3D pipelines

## Key Concepts
- **Meshes**: Traditional vertices, edges, and faces representation
- **Non-mesh representations**: Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs), Gaussian Splatting, Triplanes
- **The ML-friendly 3D gap**: Challenges in converting between research and production formats
- **Marching Cubes**: Algorithm for converting volumetric data to meshes

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this unit, you should understand:
- Why meshes are difficult for machine learning models
- Alternative 3D representations used in current research
- The trade-offs between different representation methods
- How the choice of representation affects downstream applications

## Prerequisites
- Completion of Unit 0: Introduction and Overview
- Basic understanding of 3D coordinate systems
- Familiarity with computer graphics concepts is helpful
